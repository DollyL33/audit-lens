Audit Lens (working name)

Upload a messy spreadsheet, have it cleaned with your approval, and get a dashboard that looks at your data the way an internal or forensic auditor would.

The unique kernel

An internal and forensic auditor's eye on your data. Most "upload a file, get charts" tools stop at charts. This one also runs audit tests, starting with Benford's Law and duplicate detection, and explains in plain English where the gaps and red flags are, so the data speaks for itself.

Benford's Law: first-digit test

■ actual   — expected (Benford)   ■ unusual

Red flag: amounts starting with "5" appear about twice as often as expected. In an audit, that can mean invoices kept just under an approval limit (e.g. $5,000). Worth reviewing.
Duplicates: 3 invoices appear twice with the same customer, date and amount (e.g. INV-1043, $980). Possible double payment.

Who it's for & why it matters

Who

A small business owner (or whoever handles their numbers) with sales and returns data in Excel or CSV, who wants to find where they're losing margin or taking on risk without an audit team.

Why you

Drawn from your internal audit work: finding gaps that "help company improve their margins and also help them be compliant." Plus a chance to learn Python, Streamlit, and pandas, and to understand the code.

What's in, what waits, what's cut

Now: the proof of concept
  • Python + Streamlit app, run locally
  • CSV and Excel uploads, one file at a time
  • Cleaning steps proposed behind the scenes (AI under the hood), applied by the app, with your review and approval
  • No mention of "AI" anywhere in the app: it feels like an auditor's tool
  • Dashboard with a few basic charts
  • Two audit checks: Benford's Law and duplicates, each with a plain-English explanation
Later: worth doing, not now
  • More audit tests: outliers, round numbers, weekend/after-hours entries, invoice-number gaps
  • Compliance rule checks
  • Downloadable audit report
  • Process-efficiency insights
Cut, with reasons
  • "Any file" types (PDF, JSON…): CSV/Excel covers the real use case; other formats add work without proving the kernel.
  • Accounts and saved history: not needed for one upload → one review → one dashboard.
  • Deployment: optional for the hackathon; running locally is enough for the demo.
